# AI Data Quality and Feedback Loop Reference

Use this reference for posts about AI-generated content, data quality, synthetic web content, model training, or low-quality information entering future training cycles.

The audience may be `{{TARGET_AUDIENCE}}` when a profile is available. Keep the central idea clear and do not turn it into generic product or agency promotion unless the user asks for that angle.

## Core idea

If more online content is generated by AI, future AI systems may train on AI-generated content. A feedback loop can form in which low-quality, wrong, or outdated information compounds over time.

Use measured language. Avoid claiming that all AI-generated content is bad or that a data crisis is certain unless the user provides evidence for that claim.

## Example post

What happens when AI starts training on content written by AI?

A growing share of online content is now generated, rewritten, summarized, or polished by AI.

Some of it is useful. Some of it is accurate. But a fair amount of it is low-quality: generic AI slop, badly written explanations, wrong or outdated information, questionable code, and bad practices that keep getting repeated.

The problem is what happens next.

What is AI trained on?

A significant part of the answer is data from the internet.

That is where the feedback loop starts.

Low-quality AI content can re-enter the knowledge layer and training cycles of new models. If something is wrong, it can be repeated, reinforced, and harder to identify later.

As more content is generated by AI with less human review, data quality becomes harder to control.

The future of AI will not only depend on how much data we have.

It will also depend on the quality of that data.

## Style guidance

- Stay close to the supplied source idea instead of drifting into generic AI infrastructure.
- Keep conversational bridge lines when they make the reasoning easier to follow.
- Preserve concrete examples of low-quality content when the user provides them.
- Use qualifiers such as “some,” “a fair amount,” or “can” for broad claims.
- Keep the human-review point explicit.
- Do not add statistics, studies, or predictions unless they are supplied or verified.
